# 2 Peter 2:13 — Communal Meals and False Practice

## Biblical text

> "They will be paid back with harm for the harm they have done. Their idea of pleasure is to carouse in broad daylight. They are blots and blemishes, reveling in their pleasures while they feast with you." (2 Peter 2:13, NIV)

## Original-language observation

The word translated "carouse" or "reveling" corresponds to Strong's G2689 (τρυφή / *tryphē*), meaning luxury, decadent living, or soft living. The verbal form here suggests habitual indulgence, not a one-time event. The phrase "in broad daylight" (ἡμέρας, *hēmeras*) underscores the brazenness of the false teachers' conduct.

## External source

Aquifer Open Study Notes connect this passage to the broader denunciation of false teachers in 2 Peter 2. The Tyndale Bible Dictionary entry on "False Teachers" notes that 2 Peter and Jude share significant overlap in vocabulary and theme, likely drawing on a shared tradition of polemic against libertine antinomianism in the early church.

## Historical/cultural context

Communal meals (agape feasts or fellowship meals) were central to early Christian worship (cf. Acts 2:46, 1 Corinthians 11:17-34). False teachers exploiting these meals for personal gain echoes the prophetic tradition of denouncing corrupt leaders who feast on the flock (Ezekiel 34:1-10).

## User synthesis

2 Peter 2:13 reveals a pattern where false teachers exploit communal Christian hospitality for self-indulgence. The brazenness ("in broad daylight") suggests these are not secret sins but open violations of community trust. This connects to the broader biblical theme that leadership corruption is especially damaging when it occurs within covenant community settings.

## Application

When evaluating teaching or leadership, consider not just doctrinal content but also conduct within community settings. Leaders who consume communal resources while living in decadence contradict the self-giving model of Christ (Philippians 2:5-8). This passage calls communities to discernment about the character of those who teach and lead.
